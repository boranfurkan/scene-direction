# The idea, the device, the live thing

## 1. The idea comes from the subject

Write this sentence before any code, and fill both blanks from the subject itself:

> On this site, **[a thing that belongs to the subject]** becomes **[a 3D device]**.

Look for the first blank in what is real about the subject:

- **Its object.** What it makes or is: the shoe, the bowl, the hut, the laptop the engineer works on. Model *that*, not a symbol for it.
- **Its material.** Clay, mesh and foam, timber and snow, aluminium. Material is what 3D can show that a photo can't: how light moves across it.
- **Its process or ritual.** Throwing on a wheel, a firing, a hike up, a deploy. The scroll can perform the sequence.
- **Its place.** The valley, the street, the city skyline. A diorama or a flythrough can put the visitor there.
- **Its artefacts.** Stickers, tickets, labels, receipts, a terminal, a route map. Small real things make a world believable.
- **Its name.** Letters that can be built, stacked, carved or stamped.

**The competitor test:** put the nearest competitor's name into the sentence (another frame builder, another record label, another garden). If the sentence still works, the idea belongs to the category, not to this subject. Change the first blank until it fails for the competitor.

Not ideas: "a glowing orb that breathes", "particles that react to the mouse", "a sleek 3D hero". Those are effects. An effect can serve an idea, but it never replaces one.

Examples:
- A designer-engineer's portfolio becomes **a design tool**. The page is a frame on a canvas, the name has a selection box and a size tag, a collaborator cursor drifts by, and the 3D object is his own laptop. Its lid carries stickers from his real life, it opens and types into a terminal as you scroll, and the camera flies into the screen ([furkanboran.info](https://www.furkanboran.info), see example-furkanboran.md).
- A bicycle frame builder becomes **the brazing bench**. Steel tubes and lugs come together joint by joint as you scroll, the torch's glow is the only warm light in a cold workshop, and the finished frame hangs on the wall with the owner's name painted on it.
- A record label becomes **the pressing plant**. A lump of vinyl is pressed into a record, the label is printed in the label's own typeface, and the sleeve slides over it. Each release is a record on a shelf you can pull out.
- A botanical garden becomes **the glasshouse walk**. The camera follows the path through the palm house, from the cold entrance to the humid centre, and the planted beds carry the plant names as hand-lettered tags.

## 2. Pick a device (one or two per site)

| Device | What happens | Fits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Object stage** | One hero object, lit like a product shot. The scroll turns it, tilts it, moves it between the copy. | products, tools, crafted things | an object turning for no reason; give each turn a beat (show the sole, then the heel) |
| **Exploded view / assembly** | Parts separate and rejoin, each part labelled when it's apart. | products with layers, engineering | labels that collide; fit the camera to the exploded extent |
| **Process** | The object changes state with the scroll: lump to bowl, closed laptop to open, raw to finished. | makers, services with a method | the in-between states must look good too (check the film) |
| **Fly-into** | The camera moves into the object (a screen, a window, a door) and lands in the next scene. | transitions between chapters | motion sickness: keep it short, and respect reduced motion |
| **Diorama** | A small, complete world seen from above: a valley, a street, a room, a desk. The camera drifts over it. | places, teams, stories | detail density: fewer things, better made |
| **Path / flythrough** | The camera follows a route through terrain or a space (CatmullRomCurve3). | journeys, hotels, venues | cheap terrain; use fog in the page colour for depth |
| **Material lab** | One shape, and the visitor swaps material or colourway; light shows the difference. | colourways, finishes, fabrics | swatches that are not the real materials |
| **Typographic space** | The name or headline is built as 3D type the camera moves around, or real objects spell it. | studios, events, bold brands | legibility at every angle; keep a flat readable version |
| **Playground** | One physical toy: drag stickers, stack pieces, throw a ball (one verb). | playful brands, portfolios | it must work with a thumb on a phone |

Name the device in the idea sentence, and tie each device to a section of the story: the exploded view carries the specs section, the process carries the method section.

## 3. One live thing

Give the site one thing the visitor can *do* that comes from the idea, not from the library:

- swap the shoe's colourway and watch the light change on the new mesh
- spin the wheel and the bowl's rim responds
- drag a sticker off the laptop lid (and its link works)
- move the time of day over the valley and the hut's windows light up at 19:00

Rules: it uses the subject's real content; it works with a thumb on a phone; it never blocks reading or scrolling; it is one thing, not a toolbox. If you can't name it in one line, it's not ready.
